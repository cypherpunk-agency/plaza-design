# Prologue experience review

Current playable build, 2026-10-23. Review the experience from the world glide through the
night shift, commute, Downtown and home/rest. The redesigned morning deletion, augmentation
and first-hack curriculum is M4 and is outside this review. Existing post-sleep story may
continue; that is not the newly planned M4 sequence.

## Start here

[Open the game](https://cypherpunk.agency/?play).

For a fresh review, open that link in a private/incognito window and choose **NEW GAME**.
This gives the full opening without changing your regular browser's saved run. Keep that
private window open if you want to resume during this review.

Alternatively, [open the isolated Harbor review profile](https://cypherpunk.agency/?play=harborShift).
It uses separate progress from the campaign. To replay the whole opening in that profile,
press **Escape → NEW GAME → YES, NEW GAME**. This replaces the profile's autosave, not its
manual slots or the campaign save. Do not use Continue when judging the first-time introduction.

Desktop controls: **←/→ or A/D** move; **E** interacts and advances dialogue; **Enter** also
advances dialogue while it is open; **↑/↓** use stairs/lifts where prompted; **Escape** opens
or closes the menu. Follow contextual prompts for item handovers. Touch uses the on-screen
controls in landscape. Hacking is intentionally unavailable during this ordinary working night.

There is no target completion time. Note where you feel rushed, bored or unsure; don't race.
Agents own save/door/lock/render checks. Your review is about clarity, appeal, learning and pacing.

## How to record feedback

At each checkpoint, record only what stands out:

- **Clear:** what you understood without help.
- **Confusing:** the exact place, task or line where your understanding broke down.
- **Pacing:** too fast / comfortable / dragging, and why.
- **Change:** the one alteration that would help most.

If stuck, first note what you thought the game expected. Then use the rescue route below;
using it is useful feedback, not a failed test. Don't repeatedly retry a mechanical bug: record
what happened and move on if possible. An optional screenshot helps locate the problem.

## 1. Opening glide and landing

**Do:** choose New Game; watch the glide once for this checkpoint, then take your first steps.
On repeat visits, **Escape skips the glide and lands at the playable Harbor start**. During
the initial sunrise, a key reveals the title menu rather than starting a new run.

**Look for:** a broad impression of the city followed by a clear focus on the Harbor. The
opening should establish a mood and place without demanding that you memorize the whole map.

**Tell us:** Where did you think you were going? Did you understand when control became yours?
Did the landing feel connected to the world you just saw? Was the glide too long or too quick?
Which detail stayed with you, and did anything compete with the first task?

## 2. Finding work and learning the basics

**Do:** follow the initial goal from Harbor Station into the work area. Respond to the supervisor
and begin the first delivery using only the information the game supplies.

**Look for:** movement, interaction and carrying introduced in manageable steps. You should
understand the next action without having to understand hacking, wiring or the whole district.

**Tell us:** Did you notice the goal? Did you know whom to approach and how to interact?
Did the goal animation and pings help, disappear too quickly or interrupt you? Did the boss
feel like a person in this world as well as a guide? What was the first unclear action?

## 3. Completing the night shift

**Do:** continue the delivery/return tasks, the trolley problem and checkout. Let yourself
look around naturally; don't deliberately trigger every alternate outcome.

**Look for:** enough guided practice to learn, followed by a small task you can solve yourself.
The work should suggest the world's control and inequality through ordinary experience.

**Tell us:** When did you first feel competent? Could you distinguish carrying, giving and
operating something? Did the trolley problem make sense from its feedback? Was there needless
walking or repetition? Did the reminders help without solving everything for you? Did checkout
and the wage statement make the end of the shift clear? What did the night teach you about life here?

## 4. Leaving work and travelling home

**Do:** return to the station after checkout, board the morning service and ride to Downtown.
Leave the train, then use the west stairs to reach the street.

**Look for:** a change of pace and a continuous journey. The commute can expose the city
ambiently without assigning a new lesson to every sign, person or background detail.

**Tell us:** Did you know where to go after work? Was boarding/disembarking understandable?
Did the people and train feel part of the same world? Was the journey interesting, restful
or empty? Did you understand which stop was yours? Did tunnel/platform/street transitions
feel connected? Which bit of exposition did you notice, and was there too much at once?

## 5. Downtown and arriving home

**Do:** follow the home goal to **7B**, enter and spend a moment with the ordinary home interactions
before sleeping. You do not need to inspect every object or visit every Downtown destination.

**Look for:** Downtown becoming the next place of interest while the immediate intention stays
simple: get home after work. Home should establish a small, understandable normal life.

**Tell us:** Was home easy to identify? Did the district feel inviting or overwhelming?
Did the building entrance/concierge and route into the apartment make sense? Did you want to
interact with anything at home? Did the room give you breathing space? Were you allowed to
feel like an ordinary person before the later disruption?

## 6. Rest and the prologue boundary

**Do:** use the bed and watch the rest/time transition. Stop this review when the morning
state is established; don't judge the later hacking tutorial as the redesigned M4.

**Tell us:** Did sleep feel like a natural conclusion to the night? Was the time change clear?
What did you expect would happen next? What person, place or possession would matter to you
if the game threatened it now? Does the whole prologue earn that attachment?

## Rescue route — use only if needed

Harbor Station → descend by stairs or lift → follow the quay and shift objective into the
work area. The task sequence is: supervisor's parcel → Mara → empty case back → supervisor →
try the blocked trolley → release its clamp → park trolley → return work tag → read wage statement.
After SHIFT COMPLETE, return to Harbor Station → morning train → Downtown Metro → west stairs →
Downtown → home **7B** → bed. Follow current on-screen labels when choosing an interaction.
